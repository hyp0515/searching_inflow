# Nature of Double-peak Emission Line Galaxies

## What are double-peak emission line galaxies
Double-peak emission line galaxies (DPELG or DPG) are the galaxies whose spectra show either asymmetry or double-peaked features on emission lines (Balmer's lines or forbidden lines like [OIII], [OII], etc.). Single Gaussian function usually fails when fitting these emission lines. 

These characteristics are mostly proposed to be velocity structures on galactic scale, e.g., rotating star-forming disk/ring, barred structure, or AGN outflow. Dual AGNs and galaxy mergers can also produce DP (like intuitively).

Here are some examples with clear double peaks:

## Advancement and problems to be solved in this project
1. This will be the first systematic study of DPG with data from **Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI)**.
2. Compared to previous DPG studies using SDSS, having **higher spectral resolution and much larger galaxy samples** will improve the detection rate, enlarge the DPG catalog, and then give a precious opportunity to comprehensively explore the nature of DPG.
3. We will also explore the **morphology, structural parameters, and environment of DPG as a function of velocity separation** using available imaging datasets including DESI Legacy Imaging Survey, Hyper Suprime-Cam Survey (HSC) and Euclid. The results will offer new insights into the origins of DPG and their role in our understanding of galaxy evolution.
